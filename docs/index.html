SEC 02 Embedded Systems & Hardware Engineering

The embedded subsystem runs on the resource-constrained AI-Thinker ESP32-CAM board. The engineering challenge consists of concurrently streaming high-throughput MJPEG video while driving two analog PWM micro-servos and a laser diode under a shared 5V 2A power rail.

Figure 2.0: Electrical Power Distribution & Isolated Peripheral Wiring Schematic

LEDC 14-Bit PWM Timer Mathematics

The SG90 servo motor operates on a 50 Hz PWM carrier ($T = 20 ext{ ms}$). To achieve micro-stepping accuracy without CPU-blocking software delays, the ESP32 LEDC timer is configured with 14-bit resolution:

$$ ext{Total Discrete Counts} = 2^{14} - 1 = 16,383 ext{ ticks per } 20,000 \mu ext{s}$$ $$ ext{Duty Ticks}( heta) = \left[ rac{544\,\mu ext{s} + \left( rac{ heta}{180^\circ} ight) imes (2400\,\mu ext{s} - 544\,\mu ext{s})}{20,000\,\mu ext{s}} ight] imes 16,383$$
Yields an angular resolution of 0.0135° per tick across the full 0° to 180° rotational envelope.

5V 2A Slew-Rate Limiting & Inrush Prevention

When an unconstrained hobby servo receives a sudden step command from 0° to 180°, the motor draws an instantaneous stall current of up to 800 mA. If both Pan and Tilt servos step concurrently while the ESP32 Wi-Fi radio is actively transmitting (350 mA peak), the cumulative instantaneous current demand spikes:

$$I_{ ext{peak}} = I_{ ext{WiFi}} + I_{ ext{Pan Stall}} + I_{ ext{Tilt Stall}} = 380 ext{ mA} + 800 ext{ mA} + 800 ext{ mA} = 1,980 ext{ mA} pprox 2.0 ext{ A}$$
Hardware Brownout Hazard
Drawing 2.0A on a standard 5V adapter induces an internal rail voltage sag down to ≤ 4.2V. This immediately triggers the ESP32 silicon brownout reset circuit, plunging the device into an infinite boot loop.

Mitigation Strategy: The firmware implements a 50Hz discrete S-curve slew rate limiter. Velocity is capped at 120°/second ($2.4^\circ$ per 20 ms tick). Under this controlled acceleration, the motors never enter the dead-stall electrical regime, reducing peak current by 65% to ≤ 300 mA per actuator.

SEC 03 Edge AI Vision Pipeline

High-speed aerial targets present significant challenges for standard video stream ingestion. Standard socket decoders buffer multiple frames internally. If network jitter delays packet arrival, standard OpenCV readers deliver frames that are 200 ms to 500 ms old.

Figure 3.0: FIFO Socket Buffer Stalling vs. Atomic Zero-Lag Stream Ingestion

Optical Error Coordinate Discretization

The vision model outputs bounding box coordinates $[x_{\min}, y_{\min}, x_{\max}, y_{\max}]$ along with a confidence coefficient. The aiming computer extracts the optical offset relative to the central camera boresight:

$$x_c = rac{x_{\min} + x_{\max}}{2}, \quad y_c = rac{y_{\min} + y_{\max}}{2}$$ $$\Delta x = x_c - rac{W}{2}, \quad \Delta y = y_c - rac{H}{2}$$
Where W = 320 px, H = 240 px. A positive Δx requires pan right; positive Δy requires tilt downward.

SEC 04 Robotics, Kinematics & Visual Servoing

Agesis EYE employs an **eye-in-hand** robotic configuration where the camera sensor and optical weapon are mounted directly onto the pivoting elevation C-arm. The two joints are separated vertically by a variable mechanical standoff stanchion of height $d$.

Figure 4.0: 2-DoF Kinematic Coordinate Frames & Mechanical Standoff Decoupling

Proof of Distance d Invariance

In traditional pan-tilt kinematics, the spatial offset between the joints creates geometric parallax error that forces the operator to manually retune tracking gains whenever the riser height changes.

Image-Based Visual Servoing Invariance Theorem
By driving the control loop directly from image-space optical error $\mathbf{e} = [\Delta x, \Delta y]^T$, the targeting computer decouples mechanical geometry from servo actuation: $$\Delta heta_{ ext{pan}} = -rctan\left( rac{\Delta x}{f_x} ight), \quad \Delta heta_{ ext{tilt}} = rctan\left( rac{\Delta y}{f_y} ight)$$ Because rotation around the vertical Z-axis is invariant along the stanchion line, and the elevation gimbal pivots directly around the optical center, the closed-loop convergence dynamics are strictly independent of the separation distance d.

The 3D Perspective Projection Mathematics

The digital twin renders complex multi-body geometry directly onto an HTML5 Canvas at 60 FPS without external graphical engine bloat. The perspective projection converts 3D world coordinates $[X, Y, Z]^T$ to screen pixels $[X_{ ext{screen}}, Y_{ ext{screen}}]^T$:

$$\mathbf{P}_{ ext{cam}} = \mathbf{R}_x( heta_{ ext{pitch}}) \cdot \mathbf{R}_y( heta_{ ext{yaw}}) \cdot (\mathbf{P}_{ ext{world}} + \mathbf{T}_{ ext{pan}})$$ $$ ext{Scale} = rac{f_{ ext{fov}}}{\max(50, Z_{ ext{cam}} + D)}$$ $$X_{ ext{screen}} = rac{W}{2} + X_{ ext{cam}} \cdot ext{Scale}, \quad Y_{ ext{screen}} = rac{H}{2} - Y_{ ext{cam}} \cdot ext{Scale}$$
